# HealthTracker — Agent Brief (v5)

**This version reframes the product again.** v4 described a distributable nutrition and price tracker. v5 describes an **instrument**: *track inputs, evaluate them against current research, and predict my outcomes* ([[D152]]). Where v4 and v5 disagree, **v5 wins**. Where v3 and v4 disagreed, v4 still wins over v3.

**What this does NOT change.** Everything v4 ruled about the data layer and its gates, the honesty rule, the multi-user rules, the schema, the scanner spec, the OpenFoodFacts mapping and the architecture constraints **stands unchanged**. This is a change of *direction*, not of *discipline*. D1/D3/D5/D6 stand; D2 and D4 remain retired.

## The goal ([[D152]])

> **Track inputs, evaluate them against current research, and predict MY outcomes.**

**Every slice must say which of those three it serves** — *track*, *evaluate*, or *predict*. A slice that serves none of them needs a reason before it needs a gate.

### The roadmap, in dependency order

1. **STREAMS.** Glucose now (imported from the Apple Health export, merged by timestamp). HRV, resting HR and sleep when the Oura arrives. The native shell reading HealthKit is the durable route; manual export is the stopgap.
2. **EVENT SIGNATURES + OUTCOME BITS.** Each meal tagged with what it did to glucose; each night with its HRV.
3. **AN EVIDENCE LAYER.** D32's discipline extended from lab ranges to *responses*: any claim the app makes about a pathway or a response carries its **source**, the **population studied**, and the **strength of the evidence**.
4. **PREDICTION.** n=1, from the user's own paired data, **uncertainty shown**, **opt-in**. Predict **proxy responses**; never claim to measure a pathway directly. A prediction is not a measurement, so predictions live in their own store and are never summed into or displayed as a reading — D120's separation, inherited.

### Primary outcome: vagal tone

Read through **HRV and resting heart rate**.

- **A reading's MEASURE and DEVICE are part of its identity.** Oura's RMSSD and Apple Watch's SDNN never share a series. This is the glucose-unit rule and the breath-ketone rule again, and the machinery exists: a glucose day is keyed by its unit and refuses to draw one line through two.
- **Compare against the user's OWN baseline, never population norms** — between-person spread dwarfs within-person change.
- **Ranges for labs, baseline for responses.** D32 shows jurisdictional reference ranges because that is the convention for a lab panel; a vagal-tone comparison against a population range is the same number doing a different job.

### First composite: tonicity

Inputs the app holds or will: water, sodium, potassium, alcohol, caffeine, glucose. Outcomes: day-to-day weight swings, resting HR, HRV. Anchored in calculated osmolality ≈ 2·Na + glucose + urea (mmol/L), with Na and urea from lab panels — and it **says plainly that between panels the estimate leans on intake and proxies**. The figure carries **which of its inputs were measured and which estimated**: the same number computed on a panel day and three weeks later is not the same quantity.

### Practices as experiments

Sauna, cold plunge, red light, slow breathing and similar are **logged events**. Their effect on that night's HRV against the user's own baseline is an **n=1 test, not a claim**. Contested "vagal toning" claims get **tested, not asserted**.

## What the app is

A mobile-first, fully client-side personal health instrument. No backend, no accounts, no analytics; all data lives on the device; export is always available. It remains **distributable** — no personal calibrations in code, per the multi-user rules below — but its direction is set by one person's outcomes.

Capabilities, as they actually stand:

1. **Scan → nutrients.** Barcode scan → OpenFoodFacts lookup → macros *and* labeled micronutrients → portion picker → one-tap log at `measured` confidence. The log is confirmed at the top of the screen ("Logged: <item> <amount> · <time>", Undo), as is a photo log; the same product (barcode or linked row) again within 10 minutes shows "Already logged at <time>" with Log another / Cancel and writes nothing until the tap (R279, `forms-gate`).
2. **Photo → nutrients via AI paste.** For restaurant/cooked meals: the app provides a copyable prompt template; the user sends it with their meal photo to their AI assistant (Claude or any other), pastes the returned JSON into Ingest. Macros only — see the honesty rule below. No API keys, no in-app AI calls.
2b. **Photo → named food, with the user's own key (BYOK).** The user supplies a provider key; a photographed meal or label is sent to **their** provider, which **names** the food. The numbers then come from the corpus or from remembered items, never from the model ([[D128]]: *the AI names things; numbers come from sources*). At capture only memory proposes, the matcher's top pick is never pre-selected, and every item is confirmed one at a time. Gated by `capture-gate` and `capture-outcome-gate`.
2c. **Search → cited numbers, offline.** A food typed into Search is matched against the cited composition corpus as it is typed: plural and singular find each other, as do the Canadian and US spellings (yogourt/yogurt and eight more, each measured in the shipped files); a generic row comes before a branded one; rows *headed* by the typed word come before compounds, and a word answered in the first part of a name before one answered later; a row the user has logged before leads and says so. A food found this way takes the corpus macros as its **own** numbers at `source: corpus`, cited (namespace, id, name, hash, attribution), at `eyeballed` confidence because the portion is typed; its micros stay at `reference` provenance per the honesty rule. **The corpus lives on the device** after the first download and is fetched again only when the build expects a different one — search, pick and log work with no network, including a cold start. Gated by `onedoor-gate`, `rank-gate`, `corpus-cache-gate` and `cold-start-gate`. *Known limits, stated:* on the Canadian corpus "coffee" leads with instant, brewed second, until brewed has been logged once; "sardines" leads with fish oil on both (no row is headed Sardine); a brand is recognised only by the capitals both files print trade names in.
2e. **Manual entry looks the food up (R215/4).** Typing a name in Manual brings the same cited rows as Search, calories beside each; a pick fills in calories, macros and every micronutrient the row carries for the typed grams, each still editable. Taken unchanged the item is exactly a search's (`source: corpus`, micros at `reference`); a number the user types over makes it theirs — `source: manual`, the citation kept, `adjusted` naming the changed fields, and a typed-over micronutrient becomes a **label** value removed from the reference figures, so no nutrient of one item is counted twice. Nothing matches → the from-label form (3d). Gated by `lookup-gate`.
2f. **A drink is food (R215/3).** `meal: drink`, logged like any food: **Your drinks** in + Log (read from the log, one tap = the last one again at its last size), **Log a drink** (Search, tagged), photo or manual. Calories count in the day; alcohol rides in the cited ref where the row has it. The one-tap Drink badge is retired; its old events are shown "amount not recorded". In Log a drink, rows each source files as a **beverage** (CNF group 14, SR Legacy "Beverages"; shipped in the corpus index as `groups`, with their own hash) come first **within each tier of words answered**, so "wine" leads with a wine on both databases and "red wine vinegar" still finds the vinegar (R221/3). *Known limit:* juices are filed under Fruits and fruit juices and are not promoted. Gated by `lookup-gate` and `entry-gate`.
2g. **USDA FoodData Central, with the user's own key (R242/B, v0.106.0).** Search, the not-counted picker and the photo sheet offer "Search USDA FoodData Central for ..." once the device's database has answered — Branded and Survey (FNDDS) rows, sent only on that tap, the photo sheet feeding its AI's short names (at most three). The key is entered in Settings, kept on the device only (never in an export), and shown masked. "None of these fits" comes before the rows; every row shows its kind and macros beside the item's own estimate; nothing is picked for the user. A pick is a database row like any other — its own namespace `fdcapi`, the corpus's slots, cited with FDC id and retrieval date — so the precedence rule counts it and its micros stay at reference provenance; a label's "less than" and a 0 % DV are dropped, never zero. Gated by `fdc-gate`.
2d. **Combos.** A named set of parts made, edited and deleted through one builder (`+ Log → + New combo`; "Edit" beside *My combos*). Logging a combo writes ordinary items, each a copy of its part at that moment, so editing or deleting a combo never changes a past log. A logged meal can be saved as a combo from its own block ("Save this meal as a combo", R242/2): New combo opens prefilled, nothing is written until Save, and an unsaved draft is never replaced. Gated by `onedoor-gate` and `forms-gate`.

3. **Daily log vs goals.** Daily totals of every tracked nutrient, displayed against user-configured goals (floors for things like protein and fiber, ceilings for things like sodium and kcal if the user wants them).
3b. **Served vs eaten.** A plate holds what was **served**; the day's items are what was **eaten**, and every total uses eaten. The two are shown together whenever they differ ("200 g served · ate ½ · 100 g"). The share is the user's statement — "Ate: all · ¾ · ½ · ⅓ · ¼" on the photo draft, on the Manual and Scan forms at save, and on a logged row afterwards — default *all*, never a prompt. The inedible part comes from the Canadian Nutrient File's refuse figure, cited with the row it matched: automatic only for bone on a row that is itself bone-in (or a wing, drumstick, rib or neck); peel, shell, pit and fat are one tap, off by default; the user's "boneless" / "with bone" answer is remembered per food and wins. No correction on the US corpus (no refuse source). A partly eaten meal's reminder (the day's "N leftovers" line, and Leftovers in + Log) carries one button, **End meal** (R299.1, v0.115.0): one tap records the rest as not eaten by the user, with the tap's time and the grams left (`plate.ended`), and the reminder is gone; no item or total changes, Undo as usual; older reminders end the same way. Gated by `served-gate` and `forms-gate`.
3c. **Daily stack — a plan, logged by one tap.** Settings holds the list. Each item is a **Supplement** (the default), a **Medication** or a **Food**: the first two go on the timeline with name, amount, unit and a note, and which of the two it is is stored on the dose record (`stackKind`) so a medicine can be told from a supplement later; a Food counts in the day with its calories and is a food-database row, or one of the user's own foods, at typed grams. A logged entry leads with its kind's word; entries logged before the kind was recorded are relabelled "Supplement" at render only. "+ Log → Daily stack · N of M taken" (R246, v0.108.0) is one line with **Took all**; opened, each enabled item is a checkbox row: one tap = taken now at its usual dose, the checked row showing dose and time, each editable (what the user changes is stored as `stated`); uncheck removes it with Undo; an unchecked item is **not taken** that day, and the backup's derived `stackDays` says taken (time, dose) or not taken. Took all checks what is not yet taken at one time, with one Undo. Each entry carries `stackId`. Each item carries `added_on` (with the rule that set it, `added_basis`, shown in Settings) and `removed_on` once removed -- a removed item is kept, out of the list; on a day before `added_on` it reads **"not in stack yet"**, never "not taken", on + Log and in the backup (R255, v0.109.0). Every switch -- Switch on / Switch off in + Log, today, or On / Off inside the item's Edit in Settings (R278: the Settings row has no on/off control) -- is an event with its full time, kept (R256 -- for later circadian analysis): a day the item ended switched off reads **"switched off"**, uncounted; a day with a switch shows its times behind the item's ⓘ; the backup lists the events; no history before v0.109.0. New items are added in + Log -> Daily stack -> **+ Add to stack** (shown even when the stack is empty, R281) or in Settings (R278); the join date is today unless the user gives another (never a future one), and a change to it is kept as a join edit with its time. **A supplement can be found by its Health Canada NPN** (R281, v0.112.0): the 8 digits typed (or read from a label photo through the user's own key) are looked up in the Licensed Natural Health Products Database (OGL-Canada) on the tap -- 5 taps from + Log to a saved item; the product, each ingredient's amount per unit and form, the licence status and the citation are stored with it, and each ingredient that IS a tracked nutrient counts (units taken x amount per unit). **A dose in units (R285, v0.112.1):** with a count unit (tablet, capsule, softgel, scoop, drop, mL ...) the form asks "How many?", nutrient rows are amounts "in each" unit (`per: 'unit'`), and the single "Counts toward" is not offered; an item with no per-unit data keeps its "Amount" entry, read as before. A save that cannot complete says why beside Save and keeps the input (`stackscan-gate`). Numbers read from a photo with no database match are provisional and count only after the user confirms them. An item may count toward several nutrients (`nutrients.detail` keeps each one's amount, unit, form and source); the day's total and the upper-limit check sum each nutrient across every item. Gated by `stackscan-gate`. Gated by `stackcheck-gate`. Editing the stack logs nothing. An amount said to count toward a nutrient is converted at entry by a cited factor or refused; the editor *suggests* the nutrient when the name holds one and never sets it. **A dose that counts toward a nutrient adds to that nutrient's day total** as its own "from your stack" line, with no calories (ruled R208 — this reverses R168's "a dose never reaches totals"; a dose mapped to nothing stays on the timeline only). **A cited upper intake level carries its scope as a field**: vitamin D's is about *total* intake, so the day's food + stack is compared to it; magnesium's (Health Canada) is about *supplemental* intake only, so food is not counted toward it. A limit with no scope or no citation produces no flag. A flag states the amount, the source and the scope, and never an instruction. **Every intake flag is a collapsed note** — an icon and "Intake note — tap to read", amber and never red, a 44-point target, closed again on every render (ruled R213: *flags, not nudges*); the day-level flag lives in the Micronutrients panel and on the stack row, and nowhere on the day view. On the day, the stack has a group of its own, **"Daily stack"** (R242/3), and is never filed under a meal by the clock; sittings more than three hours apart are separate groups, each with its own time. Gated by `noauto-gate`, `stacktotals-gate` and `myfood-gate`.
3d. **My foods — a food that is in neither database.** Wherever a food-database food was required (a stack Food, a combo part) the user can add their own: name, grams per serving, macros per 100 g or per serving, and whether the numbers are **from label** or **generic values** — a field (`basis`), shown on the logged row where another item says how it was entered. Stored per 100 g in `settings.foods`, ships empty, found again by name above the database's rows. **Macros only**: a label's micronutrients keep their own entry path, and a "generic" micronutrient would be the uncited number the honesty rule forbids. **In Search too (C, v0.107.0):** "Your foods" lead the list, a pick opens at the food's serving and logs the user's numbers (`source: manual`, its basis, no citation); below them, **foods logged before with no database match** (newest first, one per name, at most three): a row with numbers logs again as last time in one tap through the repeat path, with Undo, and its amount beside it is edited first when tapped (R254/1); a row with **no** numbers is never re-logged by its row — it opens the picker for its name (database, USDA, "Enter from label"), so today's entry gets real numbers, and only "Log without nutrition" saves it with none (R254/2). Gated by `myfood-gate` and `searchmine-gate`.
3e. **Backup as a file.** "Export backup" makes `healthtracker-backup-YYYY-MM-DD-vNN.json` (NN = schema number) and hands it to the share sheet, or downloads it where a file cannot be shared; closing the sheet records nothing. Settings shows "Last backup: <date>" and nothing that urges. Restore accepts the file through a file input, which only loads it — replacing data is still the user's tap, with D3's confirmation and pre-restore backup. Gated by `backup-gate` (byte-identical round trip).

4. **Glucose, as a stream.** Imported from an Apple Health export or a Shortcut-produced file, merged by timestamp, held in its own compact store outside the log — and, since R227.1 (v0.101.0, schema v16), **carried in the export/backup** under `streams` with its provenance (with steps), restored with the log; a file with no streams leaves the device's glucose untouched. The backup is the only file that leaves the phone. A collapsed day row opens into a zoomable, scrollable chart with a time axis, a tap readout, and gaps drawn as gaps. This is leg 1 of the roadmap, and the first stream.
4b. **Sensor change, with a countdown.** One tap in + Log records a new sensor (the day shows the countdown, read-only) with its **device** and **site**; the status line reads "Dexcom G7 · Day 3 of 10 · left arm", then the grace period when it is due. Wear time and grace are the manufacturer's, stored with citation and retrieval date. A second tap within ten minutes is not a second change; an existing entry's device is set only by the user's own edit, never back-filled. Gated by `sensor-gate`.
4c. **Glucose rises with no food logged before them (E-lite).** A rise of the ruled size with no eaten food in the response window is marked on the chart, passively: no prompt, no count, no badge. Opened, it offers what could account for it in a ruled order — a sensor artifact first when the rise starts from a sudden drop or falls within a day of a sensor change, then asleep or waking, then food that was not logged, then stress or other — and stores the answer as a label on the timeline. Steps (Apple Health `StepCount`, glucose's days only) are the moving covariate. **Nothing is inferred**: no stress probability, no cause in the app's voice, and "explained" is binary — magnitude matching is deferred to the full model. Serves *track* now and is the labelled set *evaluate* will need. Gated by `elite-gate`.

4d. **Meal response review (R225, the first *evaluate* slice; per SITTING since R229).** The unit is the **sitting** — items chained within 30 min, or sharing a mealId — shown once on its meal group with carbs/fat/protein/fibre summed as eaten; item rows link to it; only food outside the sitting makes it not clean; no-glucose and partial cases say so in plain words with the last import time. Opening a meal shows, where glucose covers it, the measured rise, time back to baseline and **2 h iAUC**, and beside them in a dashed **Expected** box the user's own earlier clean meals of the same food (same combo or same item names) and of similar carbohydrate (±25 % or 10 g) — median, range, n, floor three, *"not enough similar meals yet (n of 3)"* below it — with how closely carbs and the rise have tracked (ρ, n). Not judged / not clean meals say so and are not compared. Derived at render, nothing stored, no score or advice. Defers HR/HRV; assumes logged carbs and eaten share are right. Gated by `review-gate`.
4e. **No alcohol today, and Stopped eating (R226/R230/R232, v0.103.0).** Two one-tap entries in + Log. *No alcohol today* is an explicit zero on the timeline (`no_alcohol`, value 0) — told apart from a day with nothing logged in the store and the export; a drink **with alcohol** (its source's alcohol figure > 0) logged on a marked day asks "You marked no alcohol today — remove that mark?"; a source figure of 0 never asks; a drink no source speaks for is asked about once ("Does this drink contain alcohol?") and the answer is remembered per drink (`settings.drinkAlcohol`). **Alcohol-free rewards** (Settings, **off by default**) puts a gold circle on each marked day in the 7-day and month rings and the counts beside them, out of the days so far, with the current run — a **user-chosen exception to "no streaks / no reward colours", scoped to alcohol only**, and it extends to nothing else. *Stopped eating* (Now · another time) ends the eating window and is where the fasting span starts (R265.1, below); an E-lite rise after a marked stop offers "food I didn't log" last. Gated by `drinkfree-gate`.
4f. **One number-precedence rule, every path (R233.1/R234/R237, v0.104.0, schema v17).** What the user set wins; then a linked database row, at the item's grams; then the item's own estimate; an unconfirmed item with a row suggested by the user's own past counts provisionally ("suggested — tap to confirm"); otherwise it is not counted and the day says so ("1,450 cal + 3 items not counted", one tap to the list). Capture, the later resolve pass, a repeat and restore all run it, so one item gets one set of numbers. The past was recomputed automatically by the v16 → v17 migration; each changed item keeps what it was (`recomputed`). Typing a name on a photo item is Manual's lookup, as typed, drinks first; photo corrections — including those made on plates — lead the next photo. No AI calibration is applied below a ruled floor. Gated by `precedence-gate`.
4g. **Blood pressure, one chart (R251.2, v0.110.0).** In Trends: systolic and diastolic on one fixed mmHg axis at true times, the pulse pressure a neutral band between them, faint labelled lines at 135/85 (Hypertension Canada 2020 home threshold, chosen by the user; the ⓘ cites it and says the 2025 guideline uses 130/80), no verdict colour; solid/circles vs dashed/squares with a legend; gaps over 30 h not joined; a tap picks the nearest reading ("128/82 mmHg · pulse pressure 46 · Oct 20 08:10 · entered by hand") and ‹ › step one reading at a time. Zoom in / out (44 px, up to 8x) draws it wider in a frame that scrolls sideways, opened at the newest readings, the mmHg axis pinned at the left, the scroll kept across redraws (the user's request, 28 Oct). Gated by `bpchart-gate`.
4h. **Eating green, fasting orange -- one 12 h rule (R265.1, v0.111.0).** The day ring's eat lane is **green** (eating) and **orange** (fasting), each named in the ring key. From the last logged food -- or from a *Stopped eating* tapped after it, which becomes the start -- the span is orange as it runs; food logged within 12 h turns it green (part of the eating window); 12 h or more with no food is a **fast**. **What keeps a fast (R287, v0.113.1; supersedes R269 #4):** an item -- or a combo logged together, judged whole -- with net carbs <= 5 g AND protein <= 5 g for the portion logged keeps it, whatever its fat or calories (cream in coffee, butter or coconut oil in coffee, oils); alcohol always ends it; carbs or protein nobody confirmed (an AI estimate with no row, an unconfirmed suggestion, an unmeasured zero) end it; the user's own mark on an entry or a combo ("keeps my fast" / "breaks my fast", each tap kept with its time) wins over the rule except over alcohol. Limits adjustable in Settings (default 5 g / 5 g). Read on read, past fasts included; nothing stored changes. Gated by `fastrule-gate`. **No fasting question is asked anywhere**: D22's "Fasted / Ate, didn't log" and its Settings (minimum hours, the switch) are retired, the R262 20 h question was never built; past D22 answers stay in `fastLog`, and an "ate, didn't log" still keeps its gap out of the fasts. Every count and analysis reads the rule (`confirmedFasts`). A running span past 48 h is a date, not a duration, and is not drawn. Gated by `drinkfree-gate`, `weekly-gate`.
4i. **Weekly review (R262, R265.1, v0.111.0; *evaluate*).** "This week" under the 7-day row opens a full-screen review, Monday to Sunday ("so far" until Sunday), with ‹ › for other weeks; *typical* is the 28 days before the week. A headline assembled from the numbers; rings for **meal responses** (meals with a measured rise of those logged; centre the median rise, clean judged sittings only, R225's measure), **fasting** (share of the week in fasts; centre the running fast if 12 h or more, else the longest), **alcohol-free days** (gold only with the rewards switched on, the R226 exception; otherwise plain, no run) and **sleep** (hidden until any sleep exists); a coverage line (a glucose day at 70 % of its readings); a card per ring with its basis behind ⓘ; the meal card draws the median 2 h curve, this week solid and typical dashed. The one comparison word sits beside a **calculated** probability -- that a meal this week rose more than a typical one, over every pair -- shown only with n 5 a side, 40-60 % reading "no clear change" (R260). Derived at render; the only thing stored is which week was seen, and only while the optional "Last week's review is ready" line (Settings, **off by default**) is on. Gated by `weekly-gate`.
4j. **Linking rows, the pathway pack, fermented (R286, v0.113.0; *evaluate*).** An opened day item with grams and no database row offers **"Link a database row?"**: the top three rows of the find-nutrients ranking (`resolveCompute`, the panel's own), calories per 100 g beside each and beside the item's own, and how often the user's own past picks were the first row / among the three -- **calculated on the device** (n 5 floor, R261), never a figure from a record. **Nothing counts until a tap**; a tap links through the panel's pick path, so D127's state question still asks. Offered on the day view only -- **D141 stands for capture**. The weekly review lists the week's unlinked food items as one batch, with the linked share. **The pathway pack** -- built on the user's PC (HTPrivate, private), imported in Settings, checked against its own sha256, kept in its own key and **not in the backup** -- fills the **Pathways** card -- its own card, collapsed, directly below Trends (not inside it; R297) -- with the 14 groups, each record behind an ⓘ (Reactome IDs, its sentence only once reviewed, what the phone can see, raw materials against reference intakes, reviewed claims with probability and GRADE); no pathway is ever called active or measured. An opened food says **Fermented: yes / no / unknown** and where from (a reviewed lexicon rule, provisional; else its linked row's name; else unknown), Marco 2022's tier cited with its CC BY-NC licence. The corpus carries EPA (629), ALA (851), and n-3 / n-6 totals (868 / 869). Gated by `link-gate`, `pack-gate` and `check-slots`.
4k. **The context strip (R297/R298, v0.114.0; *evaluate*).** One strip at the top of the home screen shows what relates to the last action, at a **fixed height in every state** (nothing below it moves -- R297: surface what relates to the last interaction, bury what doesn't, without the screen rearranging itself). After **food logged**: the sitting, its glucose (R225's rise and time back, or none yet), the fast status, its fermented items and **"supplies N pathways ›"** -- the pathways whose raw-material lines the food's own numbers or linked row count toward, never "activates" (R286) -- one tap opens the Pathways card at exactly those. After a **stack tick** (or Took all): today's total of each nutrient it counts toward with the stack's share, the R213 intake note collapsed, "all totals ›". After **opening a food**: its database row (or "Link a row? ›"), Fermented, its pathways. It lives **3 h or until the next of those three**, × ends it; with nothing recent it shows the fast status and the latest glucose. Driven by one note on the phone (`healthtracker-context`, not in the backup); its contents are worked out once per note and again only when glucose arrives or its day or the pack changes, and re-inserted by redraws (0.1 ms). It shows; it never writes an entry. Gated by `strip-gate`.
5. **Medication label information, sourced.** On request, the US prescribing information for a medication, selected from the FDA label and stored with its citation and retrieval date. The app never says what a drug is for in its own voice.
6. **Price — data layer only.** A per-product, per-store price history is part of the stored contract, migrated and exported with everything else. **There is no capture screen and no Open Prices lookup**, and per the v5 ruling there is no plan to add them. See *Price — kept, not roadmapped* below.

**Honesty rule (ruled; refined by D120):** micronutrients enter the log from *labeled* sources — the OFF scan path or explicit manual entry from a package label — **and from the cited composition corpus, at a distinct `reference` provenance**. The rule's purpose was to keep **fiction wearing decimals** out of daily totals, and a cited corpus value is not fiction. It is, however, **not your food**: generic cheddar is not your cheddar. So a reference value is **never summed into the same figure as a labelled one without the panel saying so**, the two live in separate maps rather than behind a flag, and the coverage line distinguishes them. The rule is refined, not abandoned: what it forbids is an *uncited* number, not a *sourced* one. The AI photo path produces macro estimates at `eyeballed` confidence and never micros; the in-app prompt template must not request micros. A vision model cannot see the iron in a stew, and daily micro totals must never be fiction wearing decimals. Days whose items lack micro data show micro totals as "from N of M items" so partial coverage is visible, not implied-complete.

## Multi-user rules (all new in v4)

- **Nothing is logged without a tap, and nothing logged is non-deletable (ruled R202/R203, 2026-10-12, v0.94.0).** This **replaces** the v4 rule that an enabled auto-supplement "persists into each new day as a flagged, non-deletable item": that setting wrote an entry whenever a day record was created — at app start or a resume after midnight — stamped with that clock. It is retired. A day record is created **empty** at every creation site. A schedule or a plan may *propose*; only a tap writes. Entries the old setting added are ordinary entries marked `autoLegacy`, with their time cleared and shown as "time unknown (auto-added at app open)"; they count on their day and are excluded from every timing analysis.
- **Every entry goes through + Log (ruled R215/R216, 2026-10-14, v0.97.0).** No control outside + Log (and Settings) writes an entry, except three **answers** to something the page shows: Fasted / Ate, didn't log (a fasting gap), the answer to a glucose rise, and the side slept on (a flagged night). Restore from the trash brings back an entry rather than making one. A side entry point is removed or becomes a link that opens + Log at the right spot (the leftovers line, today's regimen). The day **shows** — the ring, water, the sensor countdown — and does not write. Starting or ending a sleep always **asks the time** (Now, the user's usual wake time from their own nights, or any time); an open sleep is **never closed by the app** (D113's auto-close retired). **Waking up is the easiest action** (R223): the ring opens + Log on one full-width "Woke — end sleep" / "Going to sleep" button — three taps from the day; one night is one record (a night typed while a sleep is open ends it); the ring key has its own button. Gated by `entry-gate`, a census that clicks every control on the page.
- **No personal calibrations in code.** The daily **stack** (`settings.stack`) is the user's own list and ships **empty**; it is never seeded from code, and nothing from a user's stack is written in this repo — records refer to "the user's stack" only (ruled R204). Quick-add presets ship **empty**; users create their own (name + nutrient amounts + default portion). Seed data is zero days, zero presets.
- **First-run experience.** An empty state that teaches the two input paths, prompts goal setup (skippable), and exposes the copyable AI prompt template. No feature assumes the user knows the JSON contract — the app teaches it.
- **Privacy is a stated feature.** README + in-app about line: all data local, no accounts, no telemetry, export-is-yours. Device location is used only when the user invokes nearby-price comparison, is sent only as an Open Prices query parameter, and is never stored.
- **Licence (ruled R269, 2026-10-25):** **no open-source licence.** No LICENSE file and no licence grant anywhere in the tree; all rights reserved; the copyright holder is to be confirmed by the user — do not choose one. The MIT LICENSE added 2026-07-11 was removed by R269; it stays in the public git history.
- **OFF etiquette:** every OpenFoodFacts / Open Prices request carries a custom User-Agent identifying the app (`HealthTracker/<version> (<repo URL>)`). Cache-first lookups are the rate-limit courtesy.
- Repo stays history-free and now fixture-synthetic forever; it is public-facing.

## Data contract (schema v2)

Item fields: `name, meal, time, kcal, protein_g, fat_g, carb_g, fiber_g, soluble_fiber_g, confidence, notes`, plus optional `barcode`, optional `micros`, and `source`.
- `meal` ∈ breakfast | lunch | dinner | snack | drink | supplement
- `confidence` ∈ eyeballed | weighed | measured
- `source` ∈ scan | ai-paste | manual | preset | supplement | corpus
  - `corpus` (ruled 2026-10-11, v0.85.0): an item found purely by search, whose OWN macros are the cited composition corpus's at the typed grams. Written in one place (`corpusOwnNumbers`); its `ref` carries the citation; its micros stay in `ref.v` and are never copied into `micros`. Additive, no schema bump — a build that does not know the value falls back to `manual` at its restore boundary. Items logged by search under v0.84.0 keep `manual` and are not back-filled.
- `micros` is an optional flat map of canonical keys → numbers: `sodium_mg, potassium_mg, calcium_mg, iron_mg, magnesium_mg, zinc_mg, vitamin_a_ug, vitamin_c_mg, vitamin_d_ug, vitamin_b12_ug, folate_ug, saturated_fat_g, sugars_g, cholesterol_mg` (extensible; unknown keys tolerated on ingest, preserved, not displayed until recognized). Only scan/manual items may carry micros per the honesty rule; ingest strips `micros` from `ai-paste` items and says so in the ingest report.
- `soluble_fiber_g` always present (0 when unknown) on every creation path.
- Items may carry `adjusted` (v0.97.0, additive, no schema bump): the field keys a database lookup filled in and the user then changed.

Day shape unchanged: `{ status, items[], water_l }` keyed by `YYYY-MM-DD` (keys validated at every paste boundary). Water: the day's figure is `day.water_l` **plus** its `water` timeline entries (R221/2, v0.98.0): each tap is an entry, listed and deletable with × + Undo; `water_l` holds what was logged before and what an import brings, untouched.

New top-level state:
- `settings`: `{ goals: { <nutrientKey>: {value, direction: "min"|"max"} }, stack: [], foods: [], combos: [], presets: [] }`
  - `foods` (v0.96.0, additive): `{ id, name, basis: "label"|"generic", serving_g?, per100: {<macro>: n} }`. Items and combo parts made from one carry `basis`.
  - Items and dose records may carry `stackKind` (`supplement`|`medication`|`food`; v0.96.0, additive). A stack item's stored `kind: "dose"` reads as `supplement`.
  - `settings.supplement` is **retired** (schema v15): its migration carries the name and any nutrients into one `stack` item and removes the setting. Stack item: `{ id, name, kind: "dose"|"food", enabled, dose?, dose_unit?, note?, nutrients?, part?, bioactive? }` — a `dose` goes to the timeline, a `food` is a food-database row at typed grams (`part`, a combo part) and goes to the day at `meal: supplement`.
  - Items gain optional `autoLegacy: true` (v15), set only by that migration and by the item boundary converting an old `_auto` item. `_auto` no longer exists in the store.
  - A timeline **dose** record gains optional `micros` (v0.95.0, additive, no schema bump): the nutrient key(s) and amount it counts toward, copied from the stack item at the tap. Summed per day in its own provenance ("from your stack"), beside labelled and reference figures and never inside either.
- `priceLog`: `{ <barcode>: { name, entries: [{price, currency, store, date}] } }` — independent of the food log (a product can be price-checked without being eaten).

**Versioning:** blob carries `version: 2`. The existing v1→v2 migration is **in-place under the stable key** — this is the versioning machinery doing the job it was built for (first real exercise of it). v1 blobs gain empty `settings`/`priceLog`, items gain `source` (inferred: `supplement` if `_auto`, else `manual`). Forward-version guard now rejects `version > 2`.

**Legacy `uha-log-v1` support: removed.** Strip `migrateLegacy`, the legacy-paste restore route, and their harness cases (fresh start, no legacy users exist). The restore boundary accepts schema v1/v2 blobs only.

**Ingest (four shapes) and import/restore semantics carry forward** from v3/D5, with version routing amended for schema v2 (version-absent → reject; v1 → in-place migrate; v2 → as-is; > 2 → reject — see the D5 amendment): non-destructive merge never overwrites non-empty days; destructive restore is confirm-gated with the D3 pre-restore backup and degraded path; per-item coercion (numbers coerced, clamped ≥ 0) and escaping at every untrusted boundary (paste, OFF, Open Prices). Escaper covers `& < > " '`.

## The AI prompt template (shipped in-app, copyable)

A short instruction block the user pastes into any AI assistant along with their meal photo. It must request: JSON only, straight quotes, the item schema above **without micros**, `confidence: "eyeballed"`, honest portion assumptions in `notes`, `soluble_fiber_g` present (0 if unknown). The template is versioned with the schema and lives in one place in the code.

## Goals display

- The daily ring becomes progress-vs-goal for a primary nutrient (user-selectable, default kcal), with a compact goal strip for the rest: current / target, direction-aware (a ceiling at 80% is good; a floor at 80% is short).
- Daily summary rolls up all macros + all micros present, each micro annotated with its coverage ("from N of M items").
- Averages keep the calendar-window definition and complete-days-only discipline. **A day closes by itself at 04:00 the next morning** (R280, read on read, nothing stored; the close tap is optional); a closed day still takes late entries, and one that looks incomplete is flagged behind an ⓘ, each average saying how many days it includes and how many are flagged. Gated by `dayclose-gate`.

## OpenFoodFacts integration (extended for micros)

`GET https://world.openfoodfacts.org/api/v2/product/{barcode}.json?fields=product_name,brands,quantity,serving_size,serving_quantity,nutriments`
- Map nutriments to the schema: macros from `energy-kcal_100g, proteins_100g, fat_100g, carbohydrates_100g, fiber_100g`; micros from their `_100g` keys where present (sodium, salt→sodium conversion, calcium, iron, potassium, vitamins, saturated fat, sugars). Missing micros are simply absent — never zero-filled (absence ≠ zero on a label).
- Portion picker (per serving / per 100 g / custom grams) scales macros and micros together.
- Cache every successful lookup (barcode → product + nutriments + fetched-at); cache-first on rescan; offline-capable.
- All OFF strings escaped, all numbers coerced and clamped. Custom User-Agent on every request.
- Product missing / offline: keep the barcode, offer manual entry; never lose the code.

## Price — kept, not roadmapped (ruled, v5)

## Standing rule: data and the AI's probability; the user decides (R260/R261)

Every **inferred** value, claim, practice, trigger, suggestion or interpretation
shown carries an explicit probability, saying what it is a probability **of**, with
its basis behind an ⓘ (evidence grade, n, own data used or "no personal data yet",
the main uncertainty). **General** (from cited evidence) and **personal** (from the
user's data; **minimum n 5**, else "no personal estimate yet") are kept separate.
GRADE is the evidence label and the probability is the estimate -- both shown,
never merged. **No "should", no priorities, no verdicts** (sole exception:
alcohol-free rewards). **No probability -> not shown as advice**; raw data always
may be. General probabilities are written into the evidence database by an agent
at authoring (model, version, date), fixed, re-assessed only as a new version;
**the app makes no AI calls for probabilities**. A probability computed from the
user's data is labelled **"calculated"**. Every probability shown is logged once
per claim per day, resolved when the data allows, and summarised in a calibration
table behind an ⓘ in Settings. "Best practice" appears only when expert agreement
and evidence strength are both high, beside the probability. Ruled 2026-10-20;
the screens that carry it are built slice by slice -- see DECISIONS R260/R261.
